# LearnerOS

## The problem

Most learning systems know what content a student has opened and what score they received, but they do not know what the student actually understands.

Textbooks present knowledge as a fixed, linear sequence even though learning is relational: a concept may depend on ideas taught elsewhere, appear across multiple chapters, or transfer across subjects and grades. Assessments then compress the learner's reasoning into a mark, discarding whether an answer revealed a competency, a partial understanding, a procedural error, or a persistent misconception.

This missing state creates problems for both students and teachers. AI tutors respond generically because they lack a reliable history of the learner's understanding. Teachers see aggregate performance after the fact, but cannot easily trace which concepts are causing difficulty, how misconceptions are evolving, or which students share similar knowledge gaps.

The central problem LearnerOS addresses is therefore not access to more content. It is the absence of a continuously updated, concept-level model of each learner's understanding that can connect evidence from their work to the structure of the curriculum.
